# Selection evidence: 19 October 2026

Calibration was not a built family in data/rebuilds.json. Maintenance and manufacturing quality already existed; this build is specifically the gage register, recall, calibration-evidence, custody and failed-calibration impact desk. The finished capital-planning and advisory-reporting builds supplied the quality reference.

Scores are judgement, not measured demand. GageList’s [pricing](https://gagelist.com/pricing/) lists MAX at US$399 a month billed annually, US$4,788 a year, for up to 10,000 gages and unlimited users. Its [location-plan calculator](https://gagelist.com/gagelist-estimate/) explicitly selects separate plans by location. Five MAX accounts yield US$23,940 annually before discounts, services or taxes. This is a high-volume, multi-site list-price scenario, not a verified invoice or a claim that a typical small manufacturer pays it. One MAX account is below the lane’s five-figure threshold. The scenario is the qualification basis and the main commercial uncertainty.

The public page lists per-location rates only, not the five-site total. The buyer’s actual number of sites, gages and current bill must be established on the call. No current NZ/AU customer invoice or live CRM purchase of GageList was verified. Searches of historical customer notes and customer/CRM snapshots returned no matching purchase evidence. Those snapshots do not establish live customer usage. No customer names appear in this build.

[TraceGauge pricing](https://tracegauge.com/pricing.php) lists Enterprise from US$10,000 annually, but a documented export path and established buyer evidence were not found. [IndySoft](https://www.indysoft.com/solutions/clients) describes subscriptions without a current public amount; historic procurement documents were not used as current pricing. [TrackAbout](https://corp.trackabout.com/) targets returnable assets, but a qualifying current public bill was not established. Salesforce Scheduler was examined and rejected for a generic booking scope that commonly depends on public scheduling screens. Learning systems were excluded because they overlap Enterprise DNA’s business.

GageList alternatives searches return vendor, review and comparison pages, including [G2 alternatives](https://www.g2.com/products/gagelist/competitors/alternatives/). The [G2 review](https://www.g2.com/products/gagelist/reviews) inspected in this run was positive overall and did not establish price dissatisfaction. No search volume or representative complaint rate was measured. The build makes no claim that the vendor cannot produce custom reports; its [Report Builder](https://gagelist.com/knowledge-base/report-builder/) is documented.

[Export guide](https://gagelist.com/knowledge-base/data-export/) confirms the gage export route. [Import guide](https://gagelist.com/knowledge-base/data-import/) explains that headings can vary with localised labels and custom fields. An actual customer export was not available. The importer uses explicit mappings and synthetic fixtures, retaining source fields and rejecting ambiguous dates. It does not claim full calibration-history migration.

The record checks use [NIST interval guidance](https://www.nist.gov/calibrations/recommended-calibration-interval) and [traceability guidance](https://www.nist.gov/metrology/metrological-traceability), with local policies explicitly distinguished from legal requirements. There is no compliance certification claim.
